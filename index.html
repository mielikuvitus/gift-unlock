Interactive demo

AR Gift Unlock

You're in the demo space. At each stop you get a clue and a 3D map of the space. Your job is to find the next QR code to unlock a new clue.

Clear all three stops to unlock the gift in AR.

  • Move your cursor to tilt the phone, just like moving a real iPhone.
  • Client's IP (logos and branding) has been removed from the demo at their request.

The zooming effect on the page is a CSS animation I made at the client's request.

Mapping the space

I used Scaniverse to capture the space the digital map is made from. I tried to clean up the scanned model, but with only 12 hours for this part I built a simple model of the room in Blender instead.

The clue copy is mine too, as I knew where the client wanted to drop the clues.

Scanning a stop

QR codes are scanned with jsQR, which reads each frame of the camera feed in the browser. I made the test QR codes with delphitools.

  • Use the DEMO only: use fake QR button to continue. It stands in for scanning the real QR code at the stop.
  • Use the location icon to check the clue again.

To preserve the user's device battery and avoid heating the device, the camera turns off after 2 minutes.

Error recovery

If the user hasn't allowed use of the camera, I wanted to make it easy for them to allow it again and get back to the experience.

  • Use the DEMO only: use fake QR button to continue.

Camera stability

While testing, my iPhone's camera crashed when the camera was stopped and started again in quick succession. To fix this, each camera start gets its own session, and any stream from an older session is stopped straight away, so only one camera feed is ever open.

  • Use the DEMO only: use fake QR button to continue.

Clue overlay

Tap the location icon again to get back to the camera view.

The clue opens as an overlay on top of the camera view. The camera is switched off while it's open, to save battery.

Modelling the coin

I researched different coins first, then made an SVG version of the design and brought it into Blender, where I built the final model.

The sounds are from freesound.org.

The client wanted a vibration when the QR code was found, but Safari doesn't support the Vibration API, so we had to drop it.

Motion design

I iterated on both rolling and falling coins before choosing the falling coins.

I tried different approaches to visualising how the coins would land. This was the simplest version, with the coins landing in a tower rather than a pile.

Performance optimization

Every 3D model is kept light so mobile performance doesn't suffer. The models are compressed with meshopt and their textures are converted to WebP at reduced sizes.

The map model relies on colour and lighting instead of large textures, which keeps it at 77 KB.

  • Each 3D view caps its pixel ratio at 2 and frees its memory as soon as you leave the screen.

Dynamic pin positions

The map and the location pin are two separate models. Each pin's position is part of the checkpoint data in the data model I was given, so the backend can move a stop's pin whenever the layout changes.

In this demo the positions are still mocked.

The Wow effect

The gift pops open in AR. I wanted the user to be able to tilt the phone and view the gift from different directions.

I learned how to use a timeline to swap the gift for the product model from this YouTube video. Thank you, Josie!

I modelled each piece of confetti individually and created the different paths they fall along. The reveal took me 3 days.

  • Move your cursor to tilt the phone and see the gift from different sides.

DISC coins

DISC coins are the client's way to gamify the experience. Users collect them at each stop and can redeem them for real product gifts.

I made the progress bar with CSS. While the points fill up, a striped repeating gradient marches across the bar.
